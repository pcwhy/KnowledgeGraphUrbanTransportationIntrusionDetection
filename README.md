This is the shared repository for our paper: Knowledge Graphs for Urban Transportation Intrusion Detection

**Abstract:**

Urban transportation intrusion detection is difficult because many compromised messages remain individually credible until they are checked against surrounding road, sensor, and signal state. We study this problem as five-way message classification over one benign class and four attack families, and we evaluate three detector families under matched access to transportation state: a local-rule baseline, a flat-feature multiclass logistic model, and a knowledge-graph detector with explicit graph reasoning. Our contribution is a two-part evaluation that combines a controlled simulator with a real-city study built from OpenStreetMap and Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) data for downtown Austin, Houston, and Dallas. In the controlled setting, both the flat-feature logistic and knowledge-graph detectors perform well, while the knowledge-graph detector preserves an explicit rule structure. In the real-city study, the knowledge-graph detector shows better portability across cities and lower inference latency. Ablation results further show that roadside sensing and topology account for most of the graph-based detector's performance.
